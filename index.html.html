Tip for Indonesian learners: In Bahasa Indonesia the verb never changes ("Saya makan kemarin" and "Saya makan setiap hari"). In English, the verb changes: I ate yesterday, but I eat every day. Look for the time clue first!

For teachers: theory and settings
  • L1 transfer and interlanguage (Lado, 1957; Selinker, 1972). Bahasa Indonesia does not mark tense on the verb, so learners often drop -ed and -s. The tip box and the feedback point this out.
  • Morpheme order studies (Dulay & Burt, 1974; Krashen, 1982). Irregular past forms and third-person -s are acquired late, so they appear in every game.
  • Input enhancement (Sharwood Smith, 1993) and noticing (Schmidt, 1990). Time clues are highlighted after each answer.
  • Focus on form (Long, 1991). Grammar is practised inside meaningful sentences and stories about Indonesian culture.
  • Variety of practice. Recognition (Then or Now?), production (Type the Verb), syntax (Build the Sentence), vocabulary recall (Verb Memory), and error correction (Story Fixer).
